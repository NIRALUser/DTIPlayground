**Ages**
- BCP ages are nominal whole months from the session label (stated in Methods).
- HBCD ages are the candidate age at the MRI visit from the HBCD release v2.0 MRI RA prep instrument
  (`mri_ra_prep_candidate_age`), in years, converted to months. All analyses used exactly these ages; the difference
  from the table used was 0.000 for all 2,293 scans. Negative values occur (minimum −1.5 months). HBCD ages are relative to full term (40 weeks of gestation), i.e.

  infants they agree within a few weeks. Gestational age is not in our data. Data, Discussion and Limitations now state
  this as a known difference and as a plausible (untested) contributor to the pooling results (see M4).

it is not proven as the cause, since gestational ages are not available. Correcting both cohorts to a common age scale

### M5. No downstream evaluation

We implemented E4 as specified (`e4_downstream.py`, CPU, fold transformers in inference).

**Design**
- Data: the 220 complete BCP scans in 5 folds by subject. For each fold, the norm, PPCA and transformer are fitted
  without that fold's subjects.
- 50 replicates. In each:
  - a random half of the subjects forms a group;
  - a δ = 0.3 between-scan-SD shift is planted in 5 random tracts;
  - each scan receives the real mask of a random donor of the same age bin and another subject. Donors are drawn from
    all BCP scans, so about half of the scans are masked, as in BCP.
- Two further scenarios: δ = 0.1, and δ = 0.1 with every scan masked. We added them because with 220 scans the power
  at δ = 0.3 is close to 100%.
- Imputers:
  - complete case;
  - tract-wise available case;
  - age-bin mean;
  - PPCA;
  - PPCA + kNN;
  - the blend of PPCA + kNN with the fold transformer under the shipped table. Its near-observed weights were fitted
    for SIREN cells; the paper says so. SIREN per scan is too slow on the CPU for 50 replicates.
  - multiple imputation: M = 10 draws from the calibrated PPCA posterior, pooled with Rubin's rules.

**Analyses**
- (i) FA and MD tract-mean trajectories (natural spline of log age, 4 df, cluster-robust SEs), compared with the
  complete-data fit: bias, CI width, coverage.
- (ii) The planted group effect: estimate relative to the complete-data estimate and to the planted shift, SE ratio,
  power and type I error.

**Fold models.** Methods now state that the PPCA used is the transformer's saved hybrid-input PPCA (about 90% of the
fold cohort, uncalibrated), with the MI SDs scaled by the calibration factors of the fold run's separate population
model.

**Results** (Section 4.10, Table 14, Fig. A3; 50 replicates per scenario):
- **Complete case** kept 114 of the 220 scans:
  - trajectory CIs were 1.33× wider than with complete data;
  - curve bias was 0.047 SD (imputers: 0.003–0.010);
  - power for a 0.1-SD group effect fell from 36% to 26%.
- **Every imputer** kept all scans and reproduced the complete-data analysis: CI width 1.00, coverage 100%, power
  98% / 34–36%.
- **Attenuation** of a planted effect was small at the real prevalence: about 2%, i.e. estimate/full 0.98 for PPCA, the
  blend and the age-bin mean.
  - With every scan masked, the age-bin mean attenuated by 6% (0.938), PPCA and the blend by about 1% (0.991–0.993).
- **Multiple imputation** from the PPCA posterior gave the same SEs as point imputation (SE ratio 0.999).
- **Advice in the paper:** at BCP-like missingness, point imputation is adequate and much better than complete-case
  analysis. With heavy missingness, prefer model-based imputation over the age-bin mean and consider multiple
  imputation.
- **Caveat:** the type I error was 5–6% for all methods, complete case included. This is due to cluster-robust SEs with
  133 subjects. Multiple imputation is now introduced in Methods and Related work.

### M6. Missing cohort-level baselines; unequal tuning budgets

- scan offset (per measure, and over the 5 nearest tracts);
- kNN across scans (k = 5–50, nan-Euclidean, as `KNNImputer`);
