# Review, round 1

**Manuscript:** "Imputing diffusion profiles of white matter fiber tracts in early brain development: an extensive
evaluation of normative, local, graph and transformer models"
**Submitted to:** Imaging Neuroscience
**Recommendation:** Major revision

---

## 1. Summary of the submission

The authors evaluate methods for imputing missing values in along-tract diffusion profiles (69 atlas tracts,
5,388 locations, 7 measures: FA, MD, RD, AD, NDI, ODI, FWF) in 423 BCP scans (208 children, 0-65 months). They hide
observed values in five synthetic patterns: interior gaps, end gaps, whole profiles, all tracts through a sphere
("region"), and a whole measure family. They then compare roughly 30 settings from six method families on identical
hidden values. The protocol uses 3 random draws of 54 scans, 5 folds by subject, and errors expressed as the RMSE
ratio to the age-bin mean (3 bins). Per-scan paired contrasts carry a subject-level cluster bootstrap and Holm
adjustment. The work is organised in the "phases" that built the DTIPlayground `impute-profiles` command. The main
findings are:

- Same-scan methods work only for gaps and ends.
- Imputing deviations from an age-bin norm makes every pattern imputable.
- A PPCA population model with SIREN on its residuals within 4 mm (0.614) is a strong baseline.
- Amortized hybrid networks (graph U-Net, transformer) are worse alone but much better for a missing measure family.
- A per-measure, per-distance weight table blending PPCA + SIREN with the transformer reaches 0.569 (0.540 with HBCD
  in the transformer's training).
- Functa, a partial VAE, age-dependent PPCA, acquisition correction, extra context inputs and seed ensembles do not
  help.
- Pooling HBCD into the norm and population model hurts.

The paper ends with practical recommendations. The authors explicitly position the contribution as an evaluation, not
as a new method.

## 2. Overall assessment

This is careful, unusually transparent work, and the right kind of paper for Imaging Neuroscience. It has several
strengths:

- All settings are scored on the same hidden values.
- Folds are split by subject.
- Paired per-scan statistics come with a subject-level bootstrap.
- The negative results are reported.
- Every number in the text is generated by scripts from the result files. I spot-checked several numbers (the
  paired ratio of the released default against PPCA + SIREN, 0.923 [0.917-0.928], and the per-pattern scores of the
  pooled runs) and they reproduce exactly from `testProfiles/ImputeValidation`.
- Tract-profile imputation has not been benchmarked before, so the paper fills a real gap.
- Several insights are of genuine practical value: the decisive role of the normative reference, the
  local/global split by distance, and that amortized networks earn their keep mainly when a whole measure family is
  missing.

The evaluation is, however, framed and weighted around the *development* of one tool rather than around the
*practitioner's* question. That limits how far the conclusions and recommendations can be trusted as general
guidance:

1. There is no untouched test set. Every choice was made on the same three validation draws, and there is no second
   test cohort.
2. The headline "overall" score gives 50% weight to gaps and end gaps. These are essentially absent from the real
   data: 168 of 204,309 profiles are partially missing, and whole-profile missingness accounts for 99.8% of missing
   cells. When the analysis is reweighted towards the real missingness, some of the recommendations change. I show
   this below from the authors' own result files.
3. The reference is a 3-bin age mean, and the oldest bin spans 10-60 months. This flatters every relative score.
4. The "pool the networks, not the population model" conclusion is oversimplified, and a relevant number (the fully
   pooled blend) is left unreported.
5. Downstream consequences of imputation are not evaluated. For a neuroimaging readership this is what decides
   whether to impute at all.
6. Cohort-level generic imputers that a practitioner would try first (e.g. `KNNImputer` across subjects, soft-impute
   or iterative SVD) are cited but not evaluated.

All of these can be addressed with the existing data and tool, most of them on the CPU. I would be glad to see this
paper again.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. No held-out test evaluation; all design decisions were made on the evaluation data.**
The hiding patterns, every hyperparameter (q = 80, the 4-mm limit, SIREN ω0 and epochs, transformer size and epochs),
the choice among blend variants (fixed, precision, table, gradient-boosted) and the shipped weight table were all
selected on seeds 0-2. Of the many contrasts run, 19 are reported with Holm adjustment.

- The cross-fitting of the weight table removes only one layer of this optimism. The authors acknowledge it
  (Limitations, first bullet) but leave a `\todo` in its place.
- Many contrasts that support the recommendations are differences of 1-2%: weight table vs fixed (0.987), 400 vs
  200 epochs (0.988), q = 40/120 vs 80 (~1.01), U-Net beyond 4 mm (0.993). Selection bias of this order is entirely
  plausible.
- Calling the 19 contrasts "pre-specified" is not accurate if they were chosen after the results were seen. Please
  say "selected".
- The 131 distinct validated scans leave about 290 scans (and their subjects) never used for validation. A fresh
  evaluation on them, plus HBCD as a second cohort, is feasible (E1, E3).
- Until this is done, the abstract and conclusions should not present 0.569 or 0.540 as estimates of out-of-sample
  performance.

**M2. The composite score does not reflect the missingness that occurs, and the recommendations depend on the
weighting.**
The "overall" score is the unweighted mean of four patterns. Section 3.1 itself shows that real BCP missingness is
almost entirely whole profiles, always all measures of a tract at once:

- 99.8% of missing cells belong to whole missing profiles.
- 168 of 204,309 profiles are partially missing.
- No scan lacks a measure family.
- From `cache/desc_missing_BCP.csv`, the median scan with rejections has **1** rejected tract (75th percentile 3,
  95th percentile 12, maximum 63). Only 14 scans have 10 or more.

Gaps and end gaps therefore make up half of the headline score but almost none of the real use case. This matters
for the conclusions. From the authors' own result files (v13/P3, paired per-scan, 162 pairs):

- **Whole profiles:** PPCA + kNN is *better* than PPCA + SIREN (geometric mean ratio 0.965, subject-bootstrap CI
  0.960-0.969, better in 90% of scan draws). Table 3 shows it too (0.675 vs 0.697). Recommendation 3 ("SIREN, or
  kNN without a GPU at about 8% more error") is therefore the wrong way round for the dominant real pattern.
- **Regions:** PPCA alone is better than PPCA + SIREN (0.990, CI 0.986-0.994; 0.759 vs 0.766 in Table 3).
- **4-mm limit:** Fig. 5c shows the PPCA + SIREN error rising to ~0.80 at 3-4 mm, above the PPCA fallback just
  beyond 4 mm (~0.74). Appendix Fig. A2 shows a 2-mm limit is better than 4 mm for whole profiles and regions. The
  4-mm limit is a compromise driven by the gap patterns.

Please:

- (a) report a score weighted by the real pattern frequencies, or simply lead with whole profiles and regions;
- (b) evaluate with realistic masks (E2);
- (c) make the recommendations pattern-specific ("if your missingness is whole tracts, ...").

The whole-family pattern, where the networks shine, does not occur in BCP after QC. The claim that it "is the norm
in cohorts where one model fit fails" needs a citation or an example, such as HBCD's failed free-water fits.

**M3. The reference (age-bin mean of three coarse bins) is weak, and relative scores overstate the benefit.**
All scores are ratios to the mean of three bins, and the oldest bin spans 10-60 months, a period in which FA and MD
still change substantially. BCP ages are integer nominal months taken from the session label (`ses-012m` → 12.0;
`cache/desc_ages_BCP.csv` has no fractional ages), which matters in the 0-3 month bin. The authors' own phase-3b
result shows that a continuous age norm alone reaches 0.91 overall and 0.87 at 0-3 months (Table A1). Consequences:

- Part of what is attributed to PPCA and the networks is absorbing the within-bin age trend. The text says this
  itself: "the population model ... absorb[s] the within-bin age trend".
- The ratios are not comparable with any study using a different reference.
- Only three rows have absolute RMSEs (Table 6).

Please:

- report the main rows of Table 3 also relative to a leave-subject-out continuous age norm (kernel or spline in log
  age), and give absolute RMSEs for all main rows (E6);
- state explicitly how BCP and HBCD ages are defined. Nominal visit age vs exact age, and chronological vs
  postmenstrual or corrected age: HBCD ranges from -1.5 months, which suggests age relative to term or due date.
  This also matters for the age-matched batch correction in M4.

**M4. The second-cohort conclusion is oversimplified, partly confounded, and the reporting is selective.**
Section 4.7's title and the fifth principal finding state that the networks should be trained on the pooled data,
but the population model should not be pooled. My checks of the v15 P and E runs show the picture is more nuanced:

- **Pooling helps where the second cohort has data.** Per age bin, pooled PPCA + SIREN vs BCP-only PPCA + SIREN
  (paired, subject bootstrap) is 0.977 [0.939-1.011] at 0-3 months, 1.057 [1.035-1.082] at 4-9 months and
  1.039 [1.025-1.052] at 10-60 months. The 11-measure phase-3b run shows the same at 0-3 months (0.546 vs 0.563).
  Pooling is neutral or helpful where HBCD is dense and BCP is thin (28 scans), and harmful where HBCD is sparse or
  differs in age composition within the coarse bins. HBCD's 0-3 month scans cluster around 1 month, which shifts
  the pooled bin mean for BCP scans of the same bin.
- **The fully pooled blend is not reported.** Pooled norm, PPCA and transformer with the fixed 0.25/0.75 blend
  reaches **0.562** (macro `\rPooledBlend` is computed but never used in the text). That is the same as the released
  BCP-only default (paired 0.998 [0.985-1.010]). It is only 1.6% worse than the "reference transformer" fixed blend
  (1.016 [1.008-1.024]). So for the configuration a practitioner would actually run, the cost of pooling everything
  is small, and it is near zero at 0-3 months (0.993 [0.971-1.011]).
- **The description does not match the evaluated configuration.** The "reference" transformer that was evaluated is
  the pooled-run transformer (dump_P). As implemented (`ReferenceModel`), it imputes from the scan's norm *in the
  pooled cohort*, and is trained on pooled-PPCA inputs. Section 3.3 says "the norm, PPCA and SIREN remain those of
  BCP". This is true of the base prediction only, so please describe the transformer's inputs precisely.
- **Confounds.** The BCP transformer was trained 400 epochs, the reference one 200 epochs. The batch correction
  assumes a shift and scale that are constant over age. With median shifts of up to +2.1 SD (RD) and a common age
  range of 0-15.8 months, this assumption should be checked, at least by showing the residual age-by-cohort
  interaction.
- **"More data helps the networks but not the linear model" (Discussion) is not tested.** The comparison changes
  the data source, not the amount of data. A learning curve within BCP would test it (O1).

Please rewrite this section and recommendation 5 to reflect the age-range dependence and the pooled-blend result.
Add the decomposition requested in E5.

**M5. No downstream evaluation.**
The motivation is downstream analysis: 203 of 423 scans (48%) miss at least one profile value and would be lost to
complete-case analysis. Yet the paper does not show what imputation does to a typical analysis. The authors note in
Limitations that imputed values are shrunk towards the norm, which attenuates effects and underestimates variance;
that is exactly the risk practitioners need quantified. For Imaging Neuroscience I consider this essential, not
optional. A concrete design that uses only the existing data and inference of the saved models is given in E4. It
would also let the authors say whether point imputation is adequate or multiple imputation (draws from the PPCA or
transformer posterior plus Rubin's rules) is needed. Multiple imputation is not discussed at all, although the
methods produce posterior SDs.

**M6. Missing cohort-level baselines, and unequal tuning budgets.**
The baselines are age-bin means, within-scan spatial interpolators and PPCA. The standard, off-the-shelf imputers
that the related-work section cites, and that a practitioner would try first on a scans × cells matrix, are not
evaluated:

- kNN *across subjects* (Troyanskaya-style, e.g. scikit-learn `KNNImputer` on the deviation matrix);
- iterative low-rank SVD or soft-impute;
- chained equations (MICE / `IterativeImputer`) on a reduced representation.

The trivial "scan offset" baseline is also missing: the norm plus the scan's mean observed deviation of the measure,
or per measure × tract group. It would show how much of PPCA's gain is a scan-wide shift (relevant to the
acquisition-correction argument in 4.6). All are CPU-cheap (E8).

The tuning budgets also differ widely, which affects the negative conclusions:

- functa: 94 screened configurations;
- partial VAE: 4;
- graph U-Net: up to 60 epochs;
- transformer: 200-400 epochs plus 8 screened variants.

The partial-VAE conclusion rests on 4 runs. The "transformer better than U-Net" contrast (0.976) may reflect the
training budget. Please report the tuning budget (configurations and GPU hours) per family in a table. Either
equalise the epochs for the U-Net (O5) or qualify the comparison.

**M7. Reproducibility and self-containedness.**

- Several claims are supported only by "software documentation" or by earlier protocols (30 scans, one seed,
  11 measures):
  - CONFETTI's original configuration (0.90);
  - the noise models;
  - the gradient-boosted and calibrated-precision blends ("about as good");
  - the acquisition offsets (+0.42 SD FA);
  - the SIREN grid;
  - the 4-mm limit.

  A journal paper must stand alone. Please give these results as supplementary tables with their protocol, or drop
  the claims. The selection of SIREN hyperparameters and the 4-mm limit, which define the default, rest entirely on
  the earlier one-seed protocol. See M2: at 2 mm the dominant patterns improve.
- The Methods need essential details, currently `\todo`:
  - acquisition, preprocessing, tractography or atlas mapping, tensor and NODDI fitting;
  - the QC criteria (QC defines the real missingness);
  - the atlas (DTI_IBISEP_Feb26), with how a toddler/infant atlas is mapped to 0-65 months;
  - HBCD release and age definition;
  - ethics and data access.
- The paper scripts depend on absolute site paths (`/NIRAL/...`) and on the run folders. Please release the
  validation result files (or at least the per-scan and per-cell dumps) with the paper. If permitted, release the
  derived profile tables too, so that every table can be regenerated.
- The phase structure is confusing. The text says "six phases" but uses 1, 2, 3, 3b, 4 and 6, with phase 5
  (functa) unnamed. Consider replacing the development chronology with a method taxonomy (own-scan / norm / cohort
  linear / amortized / blends). The phase history can go to the supplement. This would make the paper much easier
  to read for the intended audience.

**M8. Uncertainty of the released default is not reported.**
Table A2 reports calibration for PPCA, the networks, the fixed and the precision blends, but not for the released
default (table blend) or the HBCD-reference blend. These are the SDs that users will receive in their output. The
precision blend is badly miscalibrated (RMS z 1.33-1.49, 87-90% coverage). The fixed blend is conservative near
observed values (0.56). "Slightly too small" for the transformer's missing family understates an RMS z of 1.20.
Please explain how the blend SD is formed, report its calibration per pattern and per distance band, and state
whether users should rescale it (E7).

## 4. Minor issues

1. **Abstract.** It is about 380 words and dense with numbers. Check the journal limit. Lead with the practical
   findings and fewer ratios. "1.8 to 2.4 times as far off" should say "for same-scan 3-D methods".
2. **"Each round has three passes on the complete scan"** (Section 3.4). Validated scans are not complete: they need
   at least 50% observed values per measure and at most 50% missing tracts (`validate()`), and only observed values
   are hidden. Please rephrase, and state how many validated scans had real missing values.
3. **Hidden load vs reality.** Pass 1 hides about 7 whole tracts plus gaps in 40% of the remaining tracts at once,
   much more than the median real scan (1 tract). Say that this makes the protocol conservative for local methods,
   or show the error as a function of the number of simultaneously missing tracts.
4. **Equation 1.** Define the deviation-unit quantities t, b, g explicitly (z with the bin SD?). Clarify whether the
   c_i group MSEs are computed on the training folds only.
5. **Interpretation section.** It contains untested causal explanations: seed variance vs bias; context "already
   encoded"; "neighbouring tracts differ systematically by orientation". Mark them as hypotheses, or support them
   (e.g. variance across the three seed-ensemble members vs error; between-tract offsets of neighbouring tracts).
6. **Statistical reporting.**
   - p-values such as "<10^-26" add nothing. Report effect sizes with CIs, and consider a practical-equivalence
     margin (e.g. ±2%) for the "did not help" claims.
   - Note that the subject bootstrap does not capture variation from network training (one model per fold and seed).
7. **Fig. 3 (progression).**
   - The bars start at 0.3, which visually exaggerates the differences. Start at 0, or use dots only.
   - In (b), the legend overlaps the P1 rows.
   - Label rows with method names rather than phase codes.
8. **Fig. 4.**
   - The shared legend overlaps the rotated x tick labels and the axis label.
   - The distance bins (0-2, 2-4, ...) differ from those of Fig. 5c and the weight-table bands. Harmonise them.
   - Interior gaps are omitted. Show them for completeness: 60% of their cells are at 0-2 mm, but the remainder is
     informative.
9. **Fig. 5c.** Add PPCA alone, so that the cost of SIREN at 2-4 mm (M2) is visible.
10. **Fig. 8.**
    - (a, b) use the fixed blend, whereas the rest of the paper reports the released default. Use the released
      default.
    - In (c), the age-bin tick labels collide, and the FA and MD group labels sit far below the axis.
    - The kernel smooth extrapolates at 0 and above 48 months, where there are few scans. Show the confidence bands
      or the number of scans.
11. **Fig. 2.** Add anatomical orientation (L/R, A/P, S/I) and a scale bar. State the sphere radius of the draw shown.
12. **Fig. 1b.** The per-tract rejection rate is plotted against axis proximity, but no correlation is reported, and
    the figure's purpose is unclear. Either test the relation or explain why it is shown.
13. **Table 2.** The "Information" column hyphenates "co-hort" badly in the PDF. Widen the column or abbreviate.
14. **Table 3.** The `[p]` float puts the main table on a page of its own. That is acceptable, but cite it close
    to its first mention. Consider adding the PPCA + kNN row with the transformer blend, which is the CPU option for
    practitioners.
15. **Free-water measures.** They were dropped from BCP because HBCD's fits failed. Since the main analyses are on
    BCP only, justify dropping them for BCP too, or report BCP results with them in the supplement.
16. **Table 4 (screen).**
    - The "best of 94 configurations" is selected on the same test split, so the functa row is optimistic. That is
      conservative for the negative claim, but say so.
    - Hybrid functa (0.702) *is* better than PPCA alone (0.720) on the screen. The text should acknowledge this
      2.5% gain, and whether it was ever combined with SIREN or blended.
17. **Acquisition labels.** The FA offset of +0.42 SD between `shells06select` and `dir79select` is large. The age
    norm mixes the two acquisitions, whose age distributions probably differ. Report the acquisition × age-bin
    cross-tabulation.
18. **Per-tract results.** `validation_by_tract.csv` exists for all runs. A per-tract figure or table (error of the
    released default per tract for whole profiles) would directly help practitioners decide which tracts are safe to
    impute (see O7).
19. **Limitations, "reference of the score".** "Favours methods on the age-bin norm less" is hard to parse. Please
    rephrase.
20. **Wording.**
    - "The scan alone does not identify a missing tract" should read "...does not predict...".
    - "Several dozen settings of six method families": give the exact count in Methods.
21. **Self-citations.** CONFETTI (`\citep{confetti}`) and DTIPlayground are cited as software repositories with
    "todo-verify". Provide archival references (Zenodo DOI for the exact 0.9.8 release).
22. **Missing related work.** Please add or discuss the following.
    - Tractometry pipelines and along-tract statistics: BUAN (Chandio et al., 2020, Sci Rep); Cousineau et al.
      (2017, NeuroImage: Clinical); TractSeg-based tractometry (Wasserthal et al., 2020); harmonization of tract
      profiles or along-tract ComBat.
    - Infant and early-childhood white matter development and normative charts: e.g. Stephens et al. (2020),
      Lebel & Deoni (2018), Dean et al. on NODDI in infancy, Ouyang et al. (2019).
    - Normative modelling in infancy (e.g. dHCP-based work).
    - Generic modern imputers relevant to the masked-autoencoder framing: GAIN (Yoon et al., 2018), ReMasker
      (Du et al., 2024), HyperImpute (Jarrett et al., 2022), soft-impute (Mazumder et al., 2010), GRAPE
      (You et al., 2020).
    - Benchmarks of imputation and its downstream effects: e.g. Jäger et al. (2021), Shadbahr et al. (2023).
    - Spatial interpolation (kriging or Gaussian processes) as the classical counterpart of the 3-D local methods.
    - Missing data in neuroimaging-derived measures (e.g. imputation of ROI-level measures in large cohorts).

## 5. Requested analyses and experiments

### Essential

**E1. Untouched evaluation of the final pipeline (addresses M1).**

- *Run:* `job.sh` with `--validate-seed 3,4,5` (as in `experiments/requests.md` R1), but restrict the draw to
  subjects never validated in seeds 0-2. If `--validate-scans-per-bin` cannot exclude subjects, add a
  `--validate-exclude-subjects` list. The 0-3 month bin (28 scans) may be exhausted; then report the 4-9 and
  10-60 month bins only, or report the overlap explicitly.
- *Settings:* age-bin mean, PPCA alone, PPCA + kNN, PPCA + SIREN, transformer (400 epochs), fixed 0.25/0.75 blend,
  and the **shipped** weight table (not refitted).
- *Report:* Table 3-style scores and the paired ratio of the shipped default vs PPCA + SIREN.
- *What would change my view:* a ratio within about 0.01 of the reported 0.923 with an unchanged ranking would make
  the headline credible. A ratio above about 0.95, or a changed ranking of PPCA + SIREN / PPCA + kNN for whole
  profiles, would require the conclusions to be softened.

**E2. Realistic-missingness evaluation (addresses M2).**

- *Preferred run:* implement the "transplanted real mask" pass (R3 in `experiments/requests.md`). For each validated
  scan, copy the scan × tract × measure mask of a randomly drawn BCP scan with at least one rejected tract.
  Optionally match the donor's age bin, which handles possible missing-not-at-random structure by age.
- *Scale:* 3 rounds and 3 seeds, the same settings as E1. All non-network settings run on the CPU.
- *CPU fallback, if the GPU budget is limited:* re-score the existing v13/v15 results with pattern weights equal to
  the real frequencies, which is essentially whole profiles. Report the whole-profile-only paired contrasts for all
  rows of Table 7 (I computed two of them above). Also stratify whole-profile errors by tract, weighted by the
  tract's real rejection rate.
- *Change in recommendations:* if PPCA + kNN or a 2-mm limit wins under realistic masks, recommendation 3 and
  possibly the shipped `--max-distance` should change.

**E3. HBCD as a second test cohort (addresses M1; R2a in `requests.md`).**

- *Run:* the same validation on HBCD alone: `--profiles-dir v15/data/HBCD/Profiles_Clean --age-csv
  v15/hbcd_ages.csv`, 3 seeds, 20 scans per bin.
- *Settings:* age-bin mean, PPCA alone, PPCA + kNN, PPCA + SIREN, transformer (400 epochs, trained on HBCD folds),
  fixed and table blends. Use the shipped BCP table, and also a table refitted on HBCD (cross-fitted).
- *Purpose:* show whether the method ranking and the size of the gains transfer to a cohort with a different
  acquisition and age distribution and a sample five times larger. A consistent ranking would substantially
  strengthen the "practical knowledge" claim.
- If the code for transfer (R2b) does not exist, it can be optional (O4).

**E4. Downstream impact on age-trajectory and group analyses (addresses M5). CPU only, using saved models in
inference.**

- *Data:* take the BCP scans with no real missing values (220 scans).
- *Masks:* for each of R = 50 replicates, transplant real masks from the 203 scans with missing values onto these
  scans, with the observed per-scan rejection distribution.
- *Imputers:*
  - (a) complete-case: drop scans with any missing tract, which is the common practice;
  - (b) tract-wise available-case;
  - (c) age-bin mean;
  - (d) PPCA + kNN;
  - (e) the released default (transformer inference on the CPU from `v15/impute_default_transformer.pt`). Models
    must be fitted without the scans being imputed (5 folds).
- *Analyses:*
  - (i) per-tract FA and MD mean trajectories, mean-profile ~ spline(log age, 4 df) + (1 | subject), compared with
    the fit on the full data: integrated absolute bias of the curve, CI width, and coverage of the full-data curve;
  - (ii) a simulated group effect: add δ = 0.3 between-scan SD to all locations of 5 tracts in a random half of the
    subjects. Compare the estimated δ, its SE and the rejection rate at α = 0.05 across imputers, separately for the
    tracts where the effect is planted and those where it is not.
- *Optional:* multiple imputation with M = 10 draws from the PPCA posterior plus Rubin's rules, to show whether point
  imputation under-states the SEs.
- *What would change my view:* this experiment would turn recommendation 6 from advice into evidence. If imputation
  attenuates δ substantially (more than ~10%) while complete-case analysis stays unbiased, the paper must say so
  prominently.

**E5. Decomposition of pooling (addresses M4).**

- *Per-bin reporting:* report the pooled vs BCP contrasts per age bin. I computed 0.977 / 1.057 / 1.039 for
  PPCA + SIREN in the three bins.
- *Pooled blend:* report the fully pooled blend (0.562) with its paired contrasts against the reference blend and
  against the released default.
- *Runs (3 seeds), separating the two components:*
  - (i) pooled norm with BCP PPCA;
  - (ii) BCP norm with pooled PPCA (batch-corrected deviations);
  - (iii) both pooled with a continuous age norm instead of bins, to remove the within-bin age-composition effect.
- *Training-length confound:* train the BCP-only transformer for 200 epochs on the same seeds (already in v15/T),
  and report "reference vs BCP" at equal epochs, i.e. reference (200) vs T (200).
- *What would change my view:* if (i) explains the loss and (iii) removes it, the recommendation should become
  "pool only with a continuous norm, or within the reference cohort's age range", not "do not pool the population
  model".

**E6. Reference-independent scores (addresses M3). CPU.**
For all main rows of Table 3, report:

- the RMSE ratio to a leave-subject-out continuous age norm (kernel in log age; already implemented as
  `--validate-age-norms`), which can be computed offline from the dumps and the profile tables for the dumped
  settings;
- the absolute RMSE per measure.

Also report the age-bin mean vs continuous norm under the 7-measure protocol, not only the 11-measure phase-3b
protocol.

**E7. Calibration of the released default and the reference blend (addresses M8). CPU, from the dumps.**
Report, per pattern and per distance band, the RMS of error/SD and the 95% coverage of the SD that `impute-profiles`
writes for the table blend. If it is miscalibrated, give a per-band rescaling or an explicit warning.

**E8. Cohort-level generic baselines (addresses M6). CPU.**
On the same hidden values (3 seeds, 5 folds by subject), working on the deviation matrix (scans × 37,716 cells):

- scikit-learn `KNNImputer` across scans (k ∈ {5, 10, 20, 50}, distance-weighted), restricted to training-fold scans
  plus the scan being imputed;
- iterative SVD / soft-impute (rank 10-160);
- the scan-offset baseline: the norm plus the scan's mean observed z of the measure, and of the measure within the
  tract's nearest 5 tracts.

MICE on 37,716 columns is impractical. A MICE variant on tract-mean values (69 tracts × 7 measures) for the
whole-profile pattern would still be informative. These baselines locate PPCA among the standard tools and test the
claim that "a scan-wide offset is already carried by the scan's own values".

### Optional

## 6. Recommendation

**Major revision.** The evaluation is extensive, honest and technically sound in its pairing and fold design, and it
would be a useful reference for the tractometry community. The main conclusions and the practical recommendations
are not yet adequately supported, for four reasons:

- there is no held-out or external test;
- the composite score is not aligned with real missingness, and the recommended local method changes under the
  realistic weighting;
- the reference is coarse;
- the pooling conclusion is oversimplified, with one relevant result unreported.

The downstream consequences of imputation, the key question for the readership, are not evaluated. The requested
analyses are feasible with the existing data and tool, and most of them run on the CPU.
